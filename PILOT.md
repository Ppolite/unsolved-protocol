# Phase 1 Pilot

## Core question

Can UNSOLVED systematically capture and prove the gap between administrative closure and physical reality?

Everything in Phase 1 serves that question.

## Scope

Launch **10–25 live challenges** in one tightly scoped infrastructure category with objective, low-cost reverification.

A preferred first category is public streetlight outages because the physical condition is observable, administrative case trails often exist, and the definition of `done` can be narrow.

## Funding mode

Phase 1 uses **pledges only**.

- no UNSOLVED custody of challenge funds;
- zero platform fee;
- published target-escalation schedule;
- published target ceiling;
- no representation that money compounds automatically.

The pledge layer tests attention and mobilization without making financial custody part of the first protocol experiment.

## Case mix

The pilot SHOULD deliberately include:

- currently open cases;
- recently closed cases;
- repeatedly reopened cases;
- newer reports;
- older unresolved reports;
- cases where responsibility is initially uncertain.

The goal is to stress the transition from `CLAIMED_RESOLVED` to an independently observed outcome, not merely collect easy successes.

## Pass criteria

Phase 1 is successful if:

- at least 80% of cases can be assigned to the correct responsible authority;
- at least 80% can be independently reverified after official disposition;
- at least one meaningful official/physical discrepancy is captured correctly;
- two independent reviewers reach the same resolution conclusion at least 90% of the time;
- median evidence review is measured in minutes rather than hours;
- a stranger can understand the challenge, evidence, and conclusion without founder explanation;
- no pilot case requires subjective political judgment to decide whether it is complete.

## Failure is useful

The pilot should also record why verification fails.

Possible failure classes include:

- authority cannot be established;
- official data is inaccessible;
- physical evidence is ambiguous;
- location cannot be reproduced;
- Resolution Contract was poorly defined;
- reviewers disagree;
- evidence collection creates unacceptable privacy or safety risk;
- physical condition changes too quickly to verify reliably.

These failures are protocol-design data, not reasons to hide the case.

## Pilot output

At completion, publish:

- total challenges;
- resolution-state distribution;
- responsibility-state distribution;
- official/physical discrepancy count;
- reviewer agreement rate;
- median verification time;
- authority-assignment success rate;
- evidence failure reasons;
- recommended protocol changes for v0.2.
