# Governance Principles v0.1

UNSOLVED's credibility depends on applying the same published rules to supporters, critics, paying customers, and non-customers.

## Core rules

1. **Methodology before outcome.** Scoring and verification rules are published before they are applied broadly.
2. **No pay-to-improve scores.** Commercial relationships cannot directly change public scores, suppress eligible cases, or purchase favorable resolution states.
3. **Evidence over rhetoric.** Public conclusions must point to the Resolution Contract and admissible evidence.
4. **Corrections remain visible.** Factual errors should be corrected quickly without silently erasing the history of the correction.
5. **Versioned methodology.** Material changes to scoring, states, or evidence standards require a versioned repository change.
6. **Right to respond.** Identified institutions should have a documented path to submit authoritative evidence or contest factual claims.
7. **Same evidentiary burden.** Institutional submissions and citizen submissions are evaluated against the same Resolution Contract.

## Challenge publication

A challenge SHOULD NOT be published as verified fact until the platform has enough evidence to establish:

- the physical condition exists or existed;
- the location is sufficiently identified;
- institutional attribution is supported at the stated confidence level;
- the Resolution Contract is objective and reviewable.

Uncertain responsibility must be labeled uncertain rather than guessed.

## Appeals and corrections

An institution, submitter, or affected party may challenge:

- factual identity or location;
- responsibility assignment;
- evidence authenticity;
- state transition;
- scoring eligibility;
- application of the Resolution Contract.

An appeal SHOULD produce a public audit event describing:

- what was challenged;
- evidence received;
- decision;
- resulting state or score change, if any;
- reviewer or review process;
- timestamp.

Sensitive material may be withheld while still publishing the decision rationale.

## Conflicts of interest

Sponsored challenges and paying institutional accounts must be visibly distinguishable from independent verification decisions.

A sponsor may fund verification or outreach but MUST NOT control the final resolution state.

## Methodology changes

Breaking changes SHOULD use semantic-style protocol versions:

- patch: clarification or non-material correction;
- minor: backward-compatible methodology extension;
- major: change that materially alters how cases are classified or scores are computed.

## Phase 1 governance

During the pilot, manual review is acceptable and encouraged where it makes disagreements observable. The goal is not to automate uncertainty away; it is to discover where the protocol fails before scaling it.
